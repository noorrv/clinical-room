# Atlas cards: prompts for the high-resolution versions

The 15 cards in `general_surgery_christmas_cards.pdf` are about 300 × 330 px. The site shows 13 of them now at that size, as postcards beside topic titles and on the Surgery board. Spleen and Thyroid are held back because their anatomy is wrong.

Generate one image per topic with the prompts below and send them over. They replace the small cards under the same names in `src/pages/archive/cards/` and can then be shown as full-width heroes.

## What to generate

- **Size:** 2400 × 1350 px (16:9), PNG or high-quality JPEG. Anything 1600 × 900 or larger works.
- **Composition:** the anatomical plate fills the right 55–60% of the frame. Keep the left 40% as calm parchment, so the site's live title and text can sit there on wide screens. On phones the site crops towards the plate.
- **Lettering:** none in the image. The site sets the topic name in its own type, so it stays sharp and correctly spelled at every size. If you also want a lettered version for printing, make it separately.
- **Consistency:** keep the same border, palette and winter scene on every card. Only the anatomy changes.

## Shared style (paste before each topic prompt)

> A plate from a luxurious vintage Christmas anatomical atlas, 16:9 landscape. Antique cream parchment with fine paper texture and gentle foxing; a thin engraved double border in deep burgundy with small corner ornaments. Decoration stays at the edges only: a burgundy satin ribbon bow at the top centre, sprigs of holly with red berries in the top corners, a snowy pine forest and a small snow-covered cottage along the bottom edge, muted gold accents. The anatomical illustration is the clear focal point, in the manner of classic hand-painted medical atlas plates: precise, medically accurate structure, realistic proportions, fine painterly shading, visible vessels and surrounding tissues, crisp detail, no cartoon shapes, no 3D render look, no blur. Palette: deep burgundy, forest green, antique cream, muted gold. Leave the left 40% of the frame as quiet parchment for text. No lettering anywhere.

## Topic prompts

1. **Appendix:** the caecum and terminal ileum seen from the front, with the vermiform appendix arising where the three taeniae coli converge. Show the mesoappendix carrying the appendicular artery, a branch of the ileocolic artery. Optionally an inflamed, swollen appendix tip.
2. **Spleen (corrected):** a spleen, not a kidney. Show the oval, purple-red organ with its smooth convex diaphragmatic surface and notched superior border, sitting under the left ribs (ribs 9–11) against the diaphragm. At the hilum on its concave visceral surface, the splenic artery and vein run along the upper border of the tail of the pancreas, with the gastrosplenic and splenorenal ligaments. No ureter and no renal pelvis.
3. **Gallbladder:** the gallbladder on the undersurface of the liver (fundus, body, neck), the cystic duct joining the common hepatic duct to form the common bile duct, the cystic artery in Calot's triangle, and the duodenum below.
4. **Stomach:** the stomach from the front (cardia, fundus, body, antrum, pylorus) with the lesser and greater curvatures, the left and right gastric and gastroepiploic arteries, and the greater omentum hanging from the greater curvature.
5. **Small intestine:** jejunal and ileal loops on their fan-shaped mesentery, showing the arcades and vasa recta of the superior mesenteric vessels, framed by the colon.
6. **Large intestine:** caecum, ascending, transverse, descending and sigmoid colon, with taeniae coli, haustra and appendices epiploicae, and the hepatic and splenic flexures.
7. **Rectum and anal canal:** a coronal section showing the rectal ampulla, the anorectal junction, anal columns and the dentate line, the internal and external anal sphincters, levator ani and the ischioanal fossae.
8. **Liver:** the liver from the front with the right and left lobes, falciform ligament and ligamentum teres, the gallbladder fundus at the inferior border, and the inferior vena cava behind.
9. **Pancreas:** head, neck, body and tail, with the head in the C-loop of the duodenum, the superior mesenteric artery and vein passing behind the neck, and the splenic vessels along the body and tail.
10. **Hernias:** a sagittal section of an inguinal hernia, showing the sac of peritoneum passing through the deep inguinal ring along the inguinal canal, with the layers of the abdominal wall labelled by colour, not by text.
11. **Intestinal obstruction:** dilated proximal small-bowel loops above a clear transition point (for example a band adhesion), with collapsed bowel beyond it and congested, oedematous walls.
12. **Peritonitis:** the opened abdomen with inflamed, reddened peritoneum, fibrinous exudate coating the bowel loops and pus in the paracolic gutters.
13. **Abdominal trauma:** a lacerated spleen or liver with a capsular tear and subcapsular haematoma, and blood in the peritoneal cavity.
14. **Thyroid and parathyroid (corrected):** the thyroid seen from the front: two lobes joined by a narrow isthmus over tracheal rings 2–4, with correctly proportioned lobes (not oversized), the larynx and thyroid cartilage above, and the superior and inferior thyroid arteries. Include a posterior inset showing the four small parathyroid glands on the back of the lobes, and the recurrent laryngeal nerve in the tracheo-oesophageal groove.
15. **Breast surgery:** a sagittal section of the breast showing lobules, lactiferous ducts converging on the nipple, Cooper's ligaments, the fat, and the pectoralis major behind. Optionally the axillary lymph nodes in a corner inset.

## Before they go live

Check each image against its prompt: the right organ, the right relations, and no stray lettering or garbled labels. Generated images often get small structures wrong, so a doctor's quick look is worth it.
